BU'D — Interactive Events for Discord

An Arabic platform for Discord game nights. I designed a consistent hosting flow that gives each game room to feel different.

My role

Independent Product Designer · Product direction and AI-assisted development

The problem

Different games need different rules and controls. The challenge was to support that variety without asking hosts to learn a new operating flow for every activity.

Design decisions

Keep the host in control

For Question & Condition, I kept answer judgment and winner entry with the host. Participants join through Discord without another website account or tracking bot, and the same person can win again.

Put guidance beside the action

I removed separate information buttons from Mini Games cards and chose the existing start dialog for instructions. This places guidance beside the decision to play; the instruction copy remains under review.

Use feedback to guide iteration

Host observations and participant reactions informed changes to gameplay logic aimed at more competitive, enjoyable play. They guide design decisions without implying a measured uplift.

Context

BU'D brings Question & Condition and Mini Games to Arabic-speaking Discord communities. I owned the product and design decisions, directed development with ChatGPT, Codex and curated references, and reviewed the implementation.

The experience

I kept the stages around gameplay consistent. Rules and controls change inside the event screen; the surrounding hosting flow stays familiar.

Outcome and learning

BU'D is live, with published activities tried by community members. Host observations and participant reactions inform further refinement. Shipped improvements include clearer round restarts, session recovery, results and winner/prize feedback. The key lesson was to standardize the hosting flow while leaving game mechanics free to differ. A familiar next step does not require every activity to feel the same.

Screenshots show the public catalog and trials. Host feedback and participant reactions are qualitative inputs to iteration, rather than measured research outcomes.
